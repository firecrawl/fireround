# How the data stays honest

A funding feed is only as good as its worst row, so every reading is checked before it becomes one; this page lists the checks in the order a round meets them, and says where each lives. It is the long form of the [README](../README.md)'s "How it works" section; the lanes that produce the readings are in [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md).

## The rules

- **Only articles are read.** Site roots, category and tag pages, investor directories and portfolio pages (`/companies/backed-by/...`, "Companies backed by X"), company profiles on news and data sites (TipRanks `/private-companies/`, CryptoRank `/ico/`), rankings of the best-funded startups ("The best-funded HR-tech startups in the Netherlands in 2026" put a database-dated angel round on the feed as new), law-firm credential pages, and paginated listings never reach extraction, and a round whose evidence is a table row or a verbless data point rather than a sentence ("Xpeng Robotics -- $900M Series A" in a trend story on the year's biggest rounds, or a passing mention with no figure and no round named, "megarounds for Silicon Valley-based OpenAI") is a listing whatever the page calls itself, unless the page is a weekly or monthly roundup by its title; the extractor labels every page it does read (article, press release, roundup, newsletter, listing, profile, analysis) and rounds from listings, profiles, and analyses are dropped.
- **Every round is grounded.** Its evidence sentence must appear in the page text, or it goes: a sidebar, a related-posts box, and an invented sentence all fail. On a page that states several rounds, the sentence must also name the item's company, and an investor attached to the item must appear in that sentence.
- **The page says what it reports.** On a single-story page, a round more than 2x away from the headline's amount is history, not news. The evidence sentence determines `certainty`: `announced` for a closed round on the record, `reported` when a publisher says it closed but cites unnamed sources, and `expected` when it has not closed ("in talks to raise", "is raising", a fund that "weighs joining" or "is considering investing", a round a company "is closing" or is "about to complete"). Across accepted sources of the same round, the strongest supported status wins: announced, then reported, then expected; an earlier pending headline becomes context once a later source states the close. After 90 days without confirmation, an early claim is `unconfirmed` and visible only through an explicit filter. A non-announced claim from a publisher below the established tier is dropped; a claim kept from an established publisher retains its certainty instead of being presented as a closed announcement. The announcement search follows tiered early claims for up to 90 days; an on-record close upgrades the same round without resetting first-seen time or dropping its source history. Vessl AI's planned Series C, which a state fund was only weighing, was once shown as closed (2026-09-25 accuracy audit).
- **Dates come from the text, never the clock.** The round's stated date, then the page's own byline, then a date in the URL, then the search result's; a round with no date stays undated and goes to review. Nothing is ever dated to the day it was crawled: a day within a day and a half of the scrape that the page's text never writes (a CMS stamping an index or an evergreen page with the time it rendered, antidote.me's 2017 press page or FinancialIT's "Fundraising News" index; the extractor answering "today" for a page that states no date) is dropped at extraction (`offClock` in `packages/core/src/firecrawl/extract.ts`), a search result's day within a day and a half of the run is not a date either, and a social post (an Instagram repost, a LinkedIn or Facebook post) is dated only by a day its text states. A roundup, a digest, a newsletter, a column, a board, or a listicle (by the extractor's label, three or more rounds on the page, or its title, address, or host: StartupCorners' `/digest/`, CTVC's `#311`, Crunchbase's unicorn board, a "169 VCs leading rounds" listicle, a Substack or `newsletter.` host; `isListPage` in `packages/core/src/records/resolve.ts`) dates its rounds to the week or month before it (`date_precision`), never to its own day and never to a day its items state (a listicle's days can be invented), and that week or month is an upper bound: no later day replaces it and a wire day after it does not settle it. A row dated only by one list is corroborated in every lane whatever the rung, and no channel sends it until a second source stands beside it. A page that recounts rounds rather than announcing one, an executive profile, an interview, a Q&A, or a podcast write-up (`isRecountPage`; the SF Business Times' Sep 25 profile carried Assort Health's June 24 Series C as "$120M funding"), is dated the same way; and the read-back may not sharpen that week to the list's own day by citing the list (Term Sheet's Sep 23 issue is not the day of a seed announced on June 10); a page published two weeks before the window is an old story and is dropped. A day known only to its month is stored as the first of that month and shown as the month ("~ 2026-09", `approxDate` in `lib/format.ts`); a weekly roundup's day is shown with "the week before this date".
- **A surer day wins, and the row remembers where its day came from.** `date_basis` records whether a day was stated by the text or borrowed from a byline, a URL, or a search result. A stated day stands; a borrowed one yields to the earliest dated release or ranked publisher's dated URL in the same row that fits it (up to 21 days before a day, inside a week or a month), whenever that source arrives (resolve, corroboration, the judge); a far older page in the sources does not hide a fitting one.
- **A dated day only moves earlier, and a person's fix stays fixed.** A later reading (a redistribution, a republication's byline, the read-back re-reading a wire copy, a merge) never moves a day-precision day that a source dated to a later day, whatever its basis says; only a search result's day, or none, yields (`neverLater` in `lib/persist.ts`). W4 Games' Aug 25 release moved to a Business Wire redistribution URL-dated Sep 18 and then to the Sep 21 the read-back found on it; bluecore-energy's moved to a trade republication's Sep 22. A day or a stage set by `pnpm fix-records` is pinned on the row (`pinned`): no reading, read-back, merge, gap-fill, or date-settling pass moves it, and running a fix file again pins a fix it applied before pins existed.
- **A round with no amount must fit what the company verifiably raised.** A reading with no amount whose stage falls below a verified earlier round of the company (a Series A after a verified Series C, or after any verified round of $1 billion or more: OpenAI's "Series A" beside its $40 billion round), or that is known only from one weekly or monthly list, starts in review with the reason in its notes (`implausibleReading` in `packages/core/src/records/plausibility.ts`). A stated figure the read-back confirmed is evidence of its own, so a company that restarts its lettering (Groq's 2026 Series A) is not held.
- **Money is read as written.** Currency words and prefixes count (150 million US dollars is USD; NZ$2 million is NZD), a figure in parentheses is a conversion, and USD is computed at a dated rate.
- **A round the page does not letter is named by its size.** A "funding round" or "new financing" of $500 million or more is a growth round (`stageForAmount`), not Other; a Form D keeps its filing label. A row shows an unnamed round as what the page called it, or "Funding round" when the page said no more; a label the page never writes (a "Series A" an extractor guessed for a release that says only "$75 million in financing", or its "unknown") becomes the sentence's own word. A figure the page wrote into the label is the amount's, never the stage: Brahma AI's $150M round read "USD 150 million fu..." as its stage until the label lost its figure (`stageWords` in `lib/format.ts`, which every surface's label goes through).
- **A round that names no stage is kept by its amount, in every lane.** It is filed under the preset whose band holds its figure (under $10M seed, $10M to $80M Series A and B, $80M and more Series C and later; `packages/core/src/records/routing.ts`), stored as Other or Undisclosed. When the page splits a financing into equity and debt ("$45 million in equity led by Bain Capital Ventures ... $30 million in debt and equipment financing"), the equity is the round and the debt sentence is kept beside it as evidence, never counted. One with neither a stage nor an amount is dropped: every such reading measured was an award, a grant, a collaboration, or a rumor.
- **Money that is not a venture round is not one, whatever the lane.** With no stage named, a debt facility or a loan, a fund close ("final close of Fund III", "flagship fund BVP XII"), a grant or a contract, a listed company's offering (a ticker beside its name, an IPO, a private placement), a buyout or an acquisition, a charity drive, a cohort's combined total, a lender's side of a loan, property or project financing ("a $500 million commitment to Project Vault"), money taken by a public body ("Troy Housing Authority", "Government of Benin"), a bond or loan add-on or tap, a credit desk's note on a new issue ("priced 7.25% at 100", "1st lien sr sec notes due 03/15/32", "high yield new issue market"), and a borrowing entity named as the company ("Stonepeak Nile Parent LLC", "Acme Bidco Ltd", an SPV) are dropped and counted as `notARound`; a Form D issuer named as a parent, bidco, holdco, borrower, or issuer is a vehicle too. Stonepeak's $125m bond add-on led the landing page on September 24, 2026: its label was "add-on", and no rule knew the credit market's words. The history lane meets the same rules: a management buyout and a public offering once promoted there as earlier rounds. The sentence must say that money came in (a raise verb in any of the languages the feed reads); a figure in an analysis with no raise is not a round. Some of it is dropped whatever the label says (`notAStartupRound` in `packages/core/src/records/routing.ts`): a company its own sentence names as listed ("Hyperscale Data, Inc., an NYSE American listed company", a ticker in parentheses; an investor's listing does not count), a share offering or rights issue (Eco5Tech's "series D share offering, allocating all 5.4 million shares"), an Indian listed company's preferential issue or allotment or qualified institutional placement (BSE-listed Prime Industries' "preferential issue raised ₹11.86 crore at ₹42 per share"), an IPO's anchor book (AceVector's "Rs 189 crore from anchor investors ahead of its initial public offering"), and a figure that is a fund's size (Norrsken Evolve's "€62 million pre-seed fund", "a limited partner in"). With no stage named, so is a bank or an asset manager named as the company (J.P. Morgan Natural Capital), a company its sentence calls an investor ("Exergy, an Africa-focused integrated energy investor"), a signed financing or investment deal, and a headline about power, energy, or infrastructure projects (all from the 2026-09-25 store audit).
- **An X post is a lead, not a record.** The X announcements lane treats a founder's, a company's, or a lead investor's funding post as a pointer to an article, found by a search on the company and the figure and read through the pipeline like any page. A profile page is never a lead: its snippet can be any of its posts, and every profile lead measured in September 2026 was months or years old. A post lands on its own in two cases only, both as a status with its own time, naming the company and a figure: the company's own homepage links the account that posted it (a company or founder account the company vouches for; X handles and display names are free to take), or a founder's X bio links the company (its domain, a handle its homepage links, or its name beside a role such as "CEO @ Parallax") and a second, independent account (not an outlet, not the same author through a quote or repost) posts the same company and figure within twelve hours, while the founder's post is less than a day old. Either way the record is flagged and unverified, its note says what it rests on, it is dated by the post as a page date rather than a stated one (so the late re-report hold can still set it aside when the store knows the round from months before), it meets the news window, tombstones, and every other rule here, and only an article moves it to verified.
- **Investors are cleaned against the sentence.** Leads need lead language, placeholders and lenders drop, a person of a firm is the firm, one firm is one entry. The round's label counts as the sentence too: Ando's label read "pre-seed round led by Accel and seed round led by Index and Emergence" while its quote named no lead, and all three leads were demoted.
- **A headquarters is the company's.** A dateline's city is where a release was issued, and a city the quote gives an investor is the investor's: Aqua Medical's release said "led by Nashville, TN-based Relevance Ventures", and the company was filed under Nashville (`scrubHeadquarters` in `packages/core/src/records/resolve.ts`).
- **A company filed under Other takes the first sector a reading names.** A stored sector otherwise stands, but "other" is the extractor's fallback, not a finding, so a later reading, a merge, or the gap-fill that names a sector replaces it (`keptSector` in `lib/persist.ts`); nine visible companies had a round naming their sector and a company still under Other (2026-09-25 accuracy audit).
- **The company's own release states the amount.** A second reading that brings a page on the company's own domain stating a different figure replaces the row's figure with it, as the read-back prefers the release: Taktile kept a podcast write-up's $120M after its gap-fill attached the release saying $110M (`applyFill` in `lib/gapfill.ts`).
- **Only this round's investors are shown.** An investor stays on a round only when a source places it in this round, and a lead only when a source says it led: the company's existing or earlier backers listed as background, a partner, or a fund's manager are not this round's syndicate, at extraction or at the read-back. A verified row takes no investor from a reading nobody verified: an unjudged re-read and a merged duplicate never add one, and corroboration and Research add none to a row the cross-source read verified (their sources attach, and the read-back that follows a row gaining sources decides; under the single-page judge, which has no such read-back, they still add). `pnpm prune-unsupported-investors` takes off what a stored row's own verdict rejects.
- **One company is one round.** Same stage inside 45 days, any equity stage inside a week, and two dated equity readings whose amounts agree within a tenth of the larger whatever the stage labels (a company does not close two equal rounds a month apart); the surer reading names it, and a fact a row already knows is never overwritten with nothing. A day from a weekly or monthly list counts as the span before it (Higharc's July 2 release and its August list are one Series C), unless the two figures are more than twice apart (Quaise's $134M first close and its $46M final close stay two rows). A late re-report is the same round too: one named stage with amounts within a tenth is one round inside a year (Fearn's June seed in a September launch story), and inside three years when the two also share a page or a lead or one is dated only by a list (Millie's February 2025 Series A in a September 2026 roundup); an extension, or a different letter inside Series D+, is a round of its own. A re-report joins its round without re-dating it (a stated day stands; otherwise the earlier day is the round's), a history round stays history, and a merge keeps the stage that says more ("Series E" over "growth"). Every row a write touches is checked again once written, updated rows as well as new ones: Greyparrot's second Series B row was re-dated by its read-back onto the July row and nothing looked again (pre-launch audit, September 2026). The company is one company across runs too: a row remembers every name key it was seen under, and two rows under two names where one name's words are all in the other's ("Ultraviolette" and "Ultraviolette Automotive") are one round when the amounts agree and they share a source or carry one stage within a week (a one-word name of five letters or fewer needs the shared source; two different domains are two companies). A new row is checked again once written, so two lanes writing one round at once end with one row. `pnpm merge-duplicates` walks the same rule to repair rows stored before it changed, and `pnpm merge-duplicate-rounds` prints each merge before it makes it. Every merge leaves a tombstone (`round_merges`: the dropped id and the row that absorbed it), because a round id is derived from the reading (company, stage, day) and another preset's sweep that reads the same page rebuilds the dropped id: Fearn's September digest copy, merged by hand at 06:05 UTC on 2026-09-25, came back at 15:01 as a stageless row and was posted to Slack a second time. A write maps an incoming id through the tombstones (chains followed) before it looks for its row, and no channel ever sends a tombstoned id.
- **A source is about its round.** A link on a round tells a reader "this page reported this round", so a page attaches only when it is about this company (`sourceFit` in `packages/core/src/records/source-fit.ts`), and every lane that attaches a source asks it: the resolver for the sweep, watch, X, and monitor readings, corroboration, and the gap-fill agent. A job posting never attaches, however well it names the company and its latest round (an Ashby posting rode on Basecamp Research's Series C); neither does a front page (`techmeme.com/index.html`), a site search, a tag or section page, or a directory entry, and corroboration does not spend a scrape on one. An aggregator (Techmeme, Digg, Hacker News, Google News) attaches only when the headline it carried when read names the company, since its permalink is often another story's neighbour. Any other page attaches when its title, its URL, a headline stating the round's amount within a tenth ("Biodiversity AI Raises $140M"), or the evidence sentence read off it names the company by its name, a stored alias, or its domain (a generic word such as "Research" is not the name), and the company's own site always does. A roundup or a newsletter that names the company in neither its title nor its URL therefore needs a sentence that does, and one story's headline about another round (a figure, not a roundup, not about many rounds) takes no sentence at all: Entrackr's "Lumikai leads $600K pre-seed round in Demoverse" ran its next snippet underneath and was listed as a source for Sammmm's seed round. Each link keeps the title the page carried and the sentence read off it (`round_sources.field_claims`), because the shared source row keeps the first title it was written with and a front page's headline changes by the hour; a per-item link to another page on the site takes its own slug as its title, not the headline of the page that linked it (traded.co's Basecamp deal page was stored under Snorkel AI's headline). `pnpm prune-unrelated-sources` applies the rule to rows stored before it.
- **A second source must describe the same round** before corroboration takes anything from it. A row whose day may be a late page's (borrowed from a byline, a URL, or a search result, or a list's week or month) searches the past year, and a page stating the same stage and the same amount within a tenth on an earlier day is that round's own announcement: it attaches, and the read-back that follows dates the row from it.
- **A profile is written only for the company's own homepage.** A website the extractor gives is kept only when the page names or links its domain (the name plus ".com" is a guess). Candidates come in order: the record's website; the company's own site among what the store already read (a host that spells the name among its rounds' sources or its dossier's, or a host the dossier read two or more pages of, one of them its front or about page, which a portal's articles never are); the website search, grounded in what the record says the company is and written without legal suffixes ("Upwind Security", not "Upwind Security Inc."), with the name's own search when that finds no homepage at all; the links in the round's press release; and last, the name's .com, a guess that is dropped if it cannot be read, as is a host only the name's own search offered (hive.com is not HIVE, a London maker of machine brains). A host spells the name exactly (penelope.health for Penelope Health), or loosely: without its descriptor words (upwind.io for Upwind Security, krakentechnology.com for Kraken Technology Group, whose "tech" no longer reads as a news site) or with a common affix on a name of five letters or more (regentcraft.com, not thehive.ai for Hive). A loose host from the search counts only when the record describes the company and the search lists the host's front page under the whole name, and its read is told that a parent whose one unit is the record's company is a different company (xpeng.com is not Xpeng Robotics). Every candidate faces the same identity check: the homepage read answers whether it is that company before a description, logo, or brand is kept. A homepage too big for the extraction is judged on its own about page (twelvelabs.io). One that cannot be read at all is confirmed, with no profile, logo, or description, only when the round's own pages place the company on the domain (its announcement published there, or its press release linking it) and a search listing on the domain names the company today (wonder.com, candidhealth.com); either alone is not enough. A refused guess comes off the row, and when another homepage verifies it replaces the refused one, and the Alexandria facts read on the refused domain go. On the 14 top companies the daily check flagged with no verified homepage (September 2026) this found 10 and placed none wrongly; Xpeng Robotics correctly stays without one.
- **A filing is not a round.** Form D records start in review, behind a gate of corporations only, equity only, no business combinations, no single-investor small sales, no vehicles.
- **Every record is read back against its sources before it publishes.** With a model key set (`AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY`, the Vercel AI Gateway, or an OpenAI, OpenRouter, or Anthropic key), the reconcile step (`packages/core/src/llm/reconcile.ts`) hands the model the record and the text of every source together, up to four pages that Firecrawl fetched and cached, and takes back each field with the source that states it and the sentence it is stated in: the day the round was announced (a roundup's own publish day is not it), the round's amount (not a total raised to date), the label, who led and who joined, and which record names the sources place outside the round. The investor names it hands back pass the same hygiene as the extractor's before they merge, so a "funds managed by Acme" or an owner-wrapped fund the model copied off the page lands as the firm. Its two lists are the round's whole syndicate: a record investor it names in neither is unsupported and comes off the row with the note `not supported for this round`, whenever it read every source in full or the pages it read name that investor (it saw the name and did not place it in this round; Ultraviolette's existing backers rode through as participants before this rule). The name keeps its seat only when a source the model did not read, a fifth page or the tail of a page cut at 12,000 characters, could state it. It runs in place of the judge before persist and again for a row that gained sources in corroboration, under a cent a record at the default `openai/gpt-6-luna`; `RECONCILE_MODEL` picks another model, `RECONCILE=off` skips it. Without a key, the judge (`packages/core/src/firecrawl/judge.ts`) does what one page allows: it reads the record's primary page with the record in hand, about 5 credits, corrects a differently stated stage, amount, or date, takes the page's leads and participants, drops a record the page recalls as history or belongs to another company, and never sharpens a week or a month from a list page's own day. On by default on every rung; a plan override turns verification off, and then nothing publishes.
- **A company's earlier rounds are records only when their own announcement says so.** A checked dossier's prior rounds are text until each is found and read like any round: one search for its announcement and the company's own post, the page read through the same extraction, resolver, dating rules, read-back, and review rules, and kept only when the round it states is the dossier's (the same company; a day on the page; no amount a quarter off, day outside the dossier's span, or other stage; the stage, the amount within a tenth, or the day agreeing). A dossier day keeps the ordinary date slack, and an undated prior round is not dated from the company's own post unless the sentence states the day, since a migrated blog republishes old posts under a new date. No page, no row: the round stays dossier text. The row is marked history, at its real date: never delivered, never unread, not in Trending or the week's numbers, and out of the feed's default view ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#history-rounds-a-companys-earlier-rounds-each-verified-on-its-own)).
- **Known company newsrooms add leads, not exceptions.** A verified homepage and its linked sibling domains are mapped for newsroom sections. The leading companies use scrape monitors on those sections; the rest are map-diffed weekly when prominent or recently funded, monthly otherwise. Funding-looking article URLs are capped at five new pages per section and enter the same extraction, identity, read-back, visibility and 14-day delivery gates as other articles. A post that explicitly dates an older round queues a history lead for its own announcement to be checked, never a new feed row. Company timelines are refreshed slowly and again after a newly ingested round, with three passes at most in each daily source run.
- **A data provider's company record fills gaps, and its funding list is a lead.** Apollo was measured on 26 visible companies before anything of it was shown (September 2026): it matched 21, one of them a namesake on the right domain (cognition.com as "CoDev Labs"), so a record counts only when its name is the company's too. Its LinkedIn page agreed with FullEnrich's on all 13 companies both had and filled 7 more, so a page shows it (a `linkedin.com/company/` path whose slug names the company) where FullEnrich has none. Its headcount sat within a quarter of FullEnrich's on 10 of 13, so it shows only where FullEnrich and YC have none. Its logo shows only where the company has none and the image loads. Its headquarters is never shown: it placed Anthropic in Cambridge, Massachusetts and a robotics startup at a Delaware registered address, so a disagreement with ours is a note for admins (it did catch two of ours: Clay filed under Egypt, Tekever under Britain). Its funding figures are never shown either: its dates are a month at best (Firecrawl's Series B dated three weeks early) and it repeats rounds. A funding event the store lacks is a history lead, promoted only when its own announcement is found and read back ([how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md#history-rounds-a-companys-earlier-rounds-each-verified-on-its-own)).
- **A late re-report is held, not sent.** When a history or lead source (an Apollo funding event, a checked dossier's prior round, the history ledger, or a stored round whose day is stated) reports the same round, meaning the same figure within a tenth and the same stage family when both name one, dated more than 45 days (and at most three years) before a row's day that the company never stated, the row is held (`hold` on the row, the reason in its notes; `lib/late-report.ts`). A held row is never delivered on any channel and no later reading moves it: it goes to review, or stays visible as flagged when it had already gone out. A day the row's own page states yields only to a stored round whose day is stated; a provider's month holds only a borrowed day. The check runs whenever a write lands (persist), after Apollo is read (in the same sweep, before delivery), and after a dossier is saved. Assort Health's June 24 Series C came back from a September 25 SF Business Times profile as "$120M funding", and Apollo had listed it for 2026-06 four seconds before the Slack post. A person who publishes or flags the row lifts the hold.
- **Nothing old goes out as new.** No channel (Slack targets and subscriptions, webhooks, the email digest, the public daily digest) sends a round announced more than 14 days before the moment of sending (`lib/deliverable.ts`): it is refused when queued and again when sent, so a round that went stale in the queue (an email digest waits out its cadence) or was re-dated earlier after it was queued is dead-lettered with the reason. The window covers the pre-launch audit's late deliveries (6 to 8 days after the announcement: a sweep's day window, a list's week) and none of last month. The rule also refuses review, rejected, history, held, and merged-away rows.
- **Publish means the page confirmed the record.** Confirmed or corrected by the judge, dated, with an amount, above 0.75 confidence. Unjudged or skipped is flagged, which a reader sees as a half-filled circle beside the source count (Developing); below 0.5, undated, contradicted, or not news is review. Review and flagged are the machine's states and follow the evidence: a row flagged before its read-back publishes the moment the read confirms it. A reviewer's publish or reject is never overwritten.

- **The biggest names are checked again every day.** The rows a reader is most likely to look up are the ones whose mistakes get noticed, so a daily check reads the companies that matter most and flags what a reader would notice is wrong, for an admin in Review ([the daily accuracy check](#the-daily-accuracy-check), below).
- **Visible rounds are checked as coverage arrives.** A bounded recurring verification pass searches at about 1, 6, 24, and 72 hours after a round was first seen, with a durable claim and next attempt time. A company's or investor's own announcement, or a wire release, plus an independent established publication ends the schedule early; a syndicated copy is not independent coverage. Each new page must pass the same round and source checks before it attaches, then the blind read-back sees the enlarged source set. A higher-tier page that contradicts amount, valuation, stage, or a stated day flags the round with the reason instead of silently replacing the field. History, rejected rows, and review rows are not queued. A round still backed only by secondhand sources at the last check stays in Review.

Each rule is a pure function with tests in `packages/core` or `lib`.

## The daily accuracy check

The rules above run as a round arrives. Some mistakes only show once a company's rows sit together: an audit of the most prominent companies found OpenAI with no homepage, a "Series A" from a trend story beside its $40 billion round, its unlettered rounds shown as Other, no founders, a total raised far below what it raised, and Anthropic's Series B dated four years late. Code fixes and repairs followed, and the check keeps the big names right without waiting for someone to spot the next one.

Every day at 05:00 UTC (`/api/cron/accuracy`, durable in `workflows/accuracy.ts`) it reads the top 100 companies by largest single round and the top 100 by total raised among the visible ones, plus every company with a round of $100M or more, and runs these rules (`packages/core/src/records/accuracy.ts`, each with its test), one flag per company and rule:

- **No verified homepage** (high): no domain, or no homepage read has verified the domain as the company's. **No description** and **no logo** (medium).
- **Stage below an earlier round**: a round with no amount whose stage falls below a verified earlier round (high: `stageRegression`, the persist rule's own test), or a lettered round with an amount two or more letters below a larger verified lettered round (medium: misdated, like Anthropic's Series B after its Series F, or a company that restarted its lettering, like Groq). A later letter after an unlettered growth round, or a bigger later round, is the company moving on.
- **Known only from a list** (medium): a round with no amount whose only source is a weekly or monthly list, dated to its week or month or titled as a roundup.
- **Under the publish bar** after a day: a visible round never read back (high), or read back and still under 0.75 confidence (low).
- **Total raised looks low** (medium): the verified total is under half of the total in the stored Apollo record, and at least $10M under it. **A large round is missing** (medium): Apollo lists a round of $100M or more that the store, the history ledger, and the dossier all lack.
- **No checked dossier** (high; medium when the dossier predates the check), **no founders** in a checked dossier, and **founders dropped**: a new dossier that no longer names a founder the one before it named (the check remembers each dossier's founders in `accuracy_memory`).
- **Large round with no stage** (medium): stage Other or undisclosed on a round of $100M or more.
- **Possible duplicate** (high): two visible rounds of the company within 30 days whose amounts agree within a tenth, a pair the merge rule missed.
- **No lead on a large round** (low): no lead investor on a round of $50M or more.

Flags live in `accuracy_flags`, keyed by company and rule, with when each was first and last seen. A flag clears on its own the first run its condition no longer holds, and one that comes back later is new. An admin dismisses a flag with a note in Review, under Accuracy; the dismissal covers the rounds or fields the flag was about, so a new round with the same problem opens it again. The flags and the run's row on Runs are admin only: a public demo's visitor gets a 404 on Review and sees neither the row on Runs nor in `/api/v1/runs`.

Three flags have a remedy the run starts on its own, because each only adds evidence and every result still passes its own lane's check: no verified homepage runs the profile refresh (the enrichment read, trying the company's own site among its sources and dossier before the search, with the name's own search as the fallback, verified against the homepage before anything is written, and clearing nothing first); no checked dossier or no founders queues a deep dive (checked before it is saved, and never for a company dived in the last two weeks or resting after a failed or rejected dive); a missing large round goes on the history ledger as a lead (origin `apollo`) and starts the company's history pass, which writes a row only when a page states the round. Each run starts at most five profile refreshes, two dives, and three history passes, the biggest companies first; a remedy rests a week before it is tried again on the same flag, and after three tries for a homepage, two for a missing dossier, one re-dive for missing founders, or two for a missing round, the flag waits for a person. Everything else is for a person.

## Investor hygiene

Investor hygiene reads the two investor lists against the round's own evidence sentence (`packages/core/src/records/investors.ts`). Leads need lead language: a firm is a lead only when the evidence says "led by", "leads", "co-led", "lead investor", "spearheaded by", or "anchored by" of it, and a firm after "from", "joined by", "backed by", "with participation from", or "alongside" is a participant whatever the extractor called it (when the evidence has lead language but never names the firms, the first three the extractor named stay leads). Placeholders drop ("undisclosed investors", "existing investors", "a consortium of", a sentence copied as a name), along with an entry that opens on a preposition ("from the payments, fintech and enterprise technology sectors"), a generic crowd or a crowd "of" a firm ("private U.S. investors", "institutional clients of Hg"), and a lone owner-wrapped or managed-by entry, which resolves to what it wraps or manages ("Acme's scout fund" is Acme, "funds managed by Acme" is Acme). Lenders drop too: the names inside a credit-facility or venture-debt clause leave the equity round. A description of investors is nobody in particular: a lowercase article or count before it ("a syndicated group of individual investors", "two American private investors", "some business angels"), a crowd of a firm ("executives from OpenAI", "partners of DST Global"), a person described rather than named ("the ex-founder of Shutterstock"), or a lowercase crowd noun at the end ("German business angels"); a firm's own capitalized name stays ("Two Sigma Ventures", "Stanford Angels"). An owner's vehicle named through descriptor words is the vehicle ("Microsoft's venture arm M12" is M12), a family office with no name is its owner, a sentence's lowercase "the" leaves the name, and "affiliated funds of BlackRock" is BlackRock. The key ignores a possessive, an "& Co." tail, and a program word ("a16z's Speedrun accelerator" is "a16z speedrun", "Wells Fargo & Co" is not "wells fargo and"), and an investor's URL is its key with hyphens (`/investors/sequoia-capital-india`; the old `%20` URLs redirect). The shown name drops an exchange ticker ("Eli Lilly (NYSE: LLY)"), a trademark sign, a trailing legal form ("Arm Holdings Inc", "Seika Daiya Engine Co., Ltd"; a brand's "& Co." stays, and so does a form that is the only thing saying the name is a company, "Fred Olsen Ltd"), an honorific and the description before it ("Chris O'Brien Lifehouse CEO Professor Michael Boyer" is Michael Boyer), a former name in parentheses ("YZi Labs (ex Binance Labs)"), and an owner written before its investing arm ("Relativity investment arm Rel Labs" is Rel Labs). The key keeps "AI" and "Labs" on an investor, so "Sovereign's Capital" no longer lands on the "Sovereign AI" row. `pnpm repair-investors` applies the same rules to stored investors. A person of a firm is the firm ("Colin Gardiner of Yonder" is Yonder), a person formerly of a firm is the person ("Jane Doe, formerly of Acme" is Jane Doe), an angel named as an investor stays, and an affiliation is not an investor: the company before "founder" or "CEO", the manager or parent in an appositive ("managed by", "a platform created by"), and the names inside a prior-round, investor-base, partner, or customer clause (or a clause dated to a month before the round's own) leave the list. One firm is one entry within the record: two spellings, a region or fund suffix, an owner-wrapped form ("Microsoft Corp.'s M12 fund" is M12), a token-prefix form ("ARK Invest" is "ARK Investment Management"), a known short form ("a16z"), or a one-edit typo with one spelling in the evidence collapse into the fuller form, and a firm in both lists is a lead only. A verdict's roles are final on the row: a lead the read-back demoted is stored as a participant, never restored by the row's old lead link. Across runs, one company is one round when the stage matches inside 45 days, and inside a week whatever the stage (a roundup's "seed" and a press release's "pre-Series A" are the same announcement; the surer reading names it). A tranche or an extension ("Series B1", "Series B extension") beside its series is its own round when the two figures differ by more than a quarter.

## Aliases

The resolver folds spellings of one company or investor together with alias rules. `rules/aliases.json` is the seed a fresh clone starts with; aliases added from the Review page are stored in the database.

## Money fields and visibility

Every money field stands on the sentence that states it. The amount is the new money a page announces; a larger total the page states beside it (a series' running total) is kept as the financing total, never as the amount or a valuation, except that one financing announced in tranches at once is published at its total. A valuation needs its own sentence on the page, naming the company, and not placed at another round (another amount beside it, another stage, another month, "earlier this year"); otherwise it is blank. A valuation is never taken from a total. Readings of one round keep every checked valuation claim, and the row shows the one a checked sentence, then the better publisher, then the majority supports; a person's pin wins, and a read-back that finds no valuation for this round retracts the stored one.

The extractor classifies the quoted figure as primary new money, a tranche, an aggregate of different rounds, a cumulative total, a future commitment, a secondary sale, an IPO allocation, debt, a grant, or internal spending. Jev checks the exact evidence sentence independently when configured; conservative sentence rules still run without it and veto an unsafe amount even if a reader called it a round. Only primary money and a newly closed tranche count as the amount. A combined announcement keeps the announced aggregate as a financing total and names its stages without inventing each stage's amount. A secondary-only transaction, IPO allocation, or a company's own spending never becomes a round. A bare "$" stays USD regardless of the company's country or source host; only an explicit currency marker on that same figure changes it. `pnpm repair-financing` previews these rules over existing visible rows without writing; `--reground` re-reads candidate release pages through Firecrawl, and changes an amount only when the page's date and stage align with the row and its sentence states primary new money. `--apply` writes only the grounded decisions.

A funding database, a profile page, or a forum (or a page the classifier read as one, on a host no tier names) never makes a round visible alone: such a row waits in Review until a company or investor post, a wire release, or a publication states it. A row that breaks an invariant goes to Review with the reason: a valuation below the amount, an amount at or above the financing total it is part of, or a second round of the same stage within 60 days with no extension or tranche between them. `pnpm repair-money` replays stored rows under these rules (a dry run unless `--apply`).

**Every round passes a publication audit before anyone sees it.** With `publication_audit` on in settings and a model key set, a database trigger keeps any row that asks to be published or flagged in review until the publication audit (`lib/publication-audit.ts`) has judged the row exactly as a visitor sees it, company profile included, against its best pages: the company's own, a wire or filing, an established outlet. It runs on a different model family from the read-back, which is blind to the record, so the two do not share blind spots. A namesake's website, a financing that is not a private company's round (a listed company's offering or placement, debt or project finance, a grant, a stake sale, a fund close, a filing alone), or a wrong amount or currency keeps the row hidden with its reasons. A total to date or a figure that includes secondary or debt moves beside the round, a closing a credible page calls still in progress becomes expected, a day the pages state replaces a borrowed one, and pages about another round leave the row; each correction quotes the sentence that proves it. Any later change to the row's company, stage, amount, scope, day, or certainty sends it back through. A person's publish counts as its audit. `pnpm bench-publication-audit` scores the audit against hand-checked rounds; on 54 rounds checked by hand on 2026-10-04 it held no clean round and caught every wrong company, every non-round, every overstated certainty, and every currency error.

A successful read-back of a single page establishes only that the record follows that page. Visibility additionally needs a company or investor publication, wire or regulatory filing, one established publication in the publisher tiers, or two independent registrable domains with distinct reporting. A low-tier single-source row waits in Review; the verification ledger starts its search immediately and publishes it automatically only after the earliest-report search and, where sources grew, a read-back of both pages. A same-name company never inherits another company's round without agreeing on its domain, headquarters, description, or a source link to the company's own site. A round held because its company's identity was in doubt is released once a page on the company's own domain states it (Firecrawl's Series A, held until its own press release counted). `pnpm repair-trust-freshness` previews what the current rules would hide, re-date, and move to history on an existing store; only `--apply` changes rows.

## The reconcile step and the judge

A verified day that is more than 14 days before the row was first seen makes the round history, including a reviewer-pinned day. This applies when a read-back, gap fill, merge, or correction changes a row, and to a newly stored old announcement before delivery. Only day-precision dates from the page, its stated text, or a verified dated URL count; a list's week or month does not. History stays on the company page with a "reported later" mark, never in Latest, Trending, or delivery. `pnpm repair-late-history` previews existing rows and `--apply` moves them. Every channel, including instant Slack, waits for a confirmed or corrected read-back before sending; a flagged post without one remains on the feed but sends no alert.

The read-back has two shapes and one contract: a record publishes only when a reader with the sources in hand confirms it. With a model key, the reconcile step reads every source together and returns each field with its citation; without one, the judge reads the primary page with Firecrawl alone. Both run before persist, the reconcile step runs again when corroboration adds sources, and neither overwrites a reviewer's publish or reject. Turning verification off in the coverage overrides leaves every record flagged, and nothing publishes. The reconcile step reads blind: the model is told who the company is and nothing the record holds about the round, and its reading is diffed against the record. A correction it cites stands only when its quote is on the page it cites (and, for money, states the figure); one that is not is left out and named in the notes. It reads originals before syndicated copies, and `sources_seen` counts the pages it read.

Before a new non-history round leaves Review, scheduled verification searches with Firecrawl highlights for the company and amount or stage over the prior year, and maps the company newsroom. Only an extracted, same-round, day-dated report on the company or investor site or an established publication can move its announcement date earlier; a search snippet cannot. A report older than the news window makes the row history under the existing late-history gate. A search failure leaves the row in Review for a later attempt.

## The evidence behind the rules

During development the store was audited row by row against its sources: a sample of records per preset, each read against its primary page and judged on company identity, stage, amount and currency, date given its precision, leads as leads, every source about the round, and whether the enrichment describes the article's company. Each rule above came out of a row that failed one of those checks. `evals/golden-rounds.json` holds the hand-verified rounds those audits produced, and `pnpm bench-reconcile -- <model> [<model>]` scores any models against them on identical prompts (per-field accuracy, agreement, latency, tokens, cost). The extraction reader comparison in [`evals/extractor-benchmark.md`](../evals/extractor-benchmark.md) uses live Firecrawl JSON and identical markdown snapshots for 82 labelled pages, not authored extraction responses. `pnpm bench-pipeline` replays the quote-required JSON captures by default; `--legacy` uses the earlier JSON and `--assert` checks the deterministic authored fixture. The quote-required Firecrawl extractor grounds each stated field in the page text before the resolver sees it, and the independent blind read-back remains a separate publication check.
